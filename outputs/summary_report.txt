1. DATA CHARACTERISTICS
- Target variable: Quantitative measure of disease progression one year after baseline.
- Features: Age, Sex, Body Mass Index (BMI), Average Blood Pressure (BP),
  and 6 blood serum measurements (S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6).
- Note: Features are already mean-centered and scaled in the scikit-learn dataset.

2. MULTICOLLINEARITY ANALYSIS (VIF Scores)
- A VIF > 5 indicates potential multicollinearity, while VIF > 10 is severe.
- In this dataset, several blood serum measurements exhibit severe multicollinearity:
  * s1: 59.20
  * s2: 39.19
  * s3: 15.40
  * s5: 10.08
  * s4: 8.89
  * bmi: 1.51
  * s6: 1.48
  * bp: 1.46
  * sex: 1.28
  * age: 1.22

Key Observations on Collinearity:
- s1 and s2 exhibit extremely high VIF scores (> 50 and > 30), indicating redundancy.
- This multicollinearity inflates standard error of coefficients in Ordinary Linear Regression,
  making coefficient estimation unstable. Regularization (L1/L2) is strongly recommended.

4. KEY FINDINGS & INSIGHTS
- Baseline Linear Regression test R²: 0.4526
- RidgeCV test R²: 0.4607
- LassoCV test R²: 0.4715
- Regularization (Ridge and Lasso) helps address multicollinearity and improves coefficient stability.
- LassoCV performs automatic feature selection by setting less important coefficients to zero,
  which yields a simpler and more interpretable model while maintaining comparable predictive power.
- Top predictors usually include BMI, s5 (Triglycerides), and BP.
